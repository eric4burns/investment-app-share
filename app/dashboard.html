Setups — one list, ranked, each with the reason

A setup is a name reading buy or add at a level to act on tonight (within 3% of it) with at least one more thing lining up: a record of calls with this evidence beating SPY, the weekly or daily agreeing, a followed author's zone or buy at the same price, a cup and handle, or a buy-back reading at the floor. The sector's relative strength (rank of eleven) is written into every reason as support or caution, but never counts on its own — half the list is one sector. Ranked by the record, then by how close the level is. Names that read buy with nothing else lining up fold away below. Each row leads with the three prices — buy at, sell into, wrong below — then says why in words, not a score; a dash means no level on that side is tested enough to trade.

Buying back — names you sold out of in the last year, and the watchlist; a held name's readings are on its card under Holdings calls

The sell side has a measured ladder. The buy-back side does not: no written rebuy rule beat holding on more than 19 of 36 names. So this is the readings that measured best, side by side, with what each has meant — the weekly Williams %R floor, the drawdown from the 52-week high, the followed authors' zones, your own last sale, the wash window — and in words which are there and which are not. The decision is yours.

Who I Follow

Followed accounts, graded

A call somebody you follow posted — the account, the name, the day, the price and what they said to do. It is graded exactly as the app's own calls are: the name against SPY over the same dates at 5, 21 and 63 trading days, signed by what the call claimed. A "wait for lower" is recorded as a sell, because that is the claim it makes about the next month. Nothing here is characterised below twenty graded calls. Since 2026-09-13 the nightly X pull reads each new post for a call in words (bought, adding, breakout, higher low; sold, trimming, lower first…) and records it the same night, marked auto — so the record is the app's from then on and a post deleted later changes nothing. A chart posted without words is not read.

Calls made

What it would have said since 2022

What this does, in plain words. Pretend it is the start date. Score every name on the watchlist using only what was known that day. Buy the ones that pass the method, at the next day's open. At the next rebalance, do it again: sell what no longer passes, buy what now does. Keep going to today, and see what that would have earned against just buying SPY.
It runs twice: once on your watchlist and once on a neutral basket of sector funds. Your watchlist is a list of names that already worked — that is why they are on it — so a method that buys strength looks brilliant on it whatever the method is. The sector funds could not have been picked with hindsight. The gap between the two runs is the result; the first number alone is not.
The last run for the selected method is shown below until you press Run. A run takes about half a minute.
